The story

It began as a voice loop on a kitchen counter: talk, and a local model talks back in under a second. It worked. It was also just a voice in a box, and a voice can't show you anything.

Then the question changed. What should a screen on the counter look like when the thing behind it is a model that can draw any card it wants? A dashboard is passive. A chat is a conversation. Some days you want it to show you something, and some days you just want to check that it did the right thing. Most assistants pick one of these. A kitchen needs all four, on one screen, without asking you to switch modes.

So we designed it fourteen times before writing a line of it.

Each version was a working prototype we lived with and threw away: glass, then solid; a mind map; an orbit; a photo frame; themes for every mood; a grid of squares. Every one taught us one thing. Version fourteen, the Stage with its TV, is the one we built: three columns, live tiles that keep their place, and a chat bar that turns into a TV when there is something to see.

Then every piece of it went into omxcode first, as a component any app can use: the Stage, the live tiles, the TV, the charms, the pointer. omx only puts them together.

  7. v9

  8. v10
    Themes and profilesA theme per person, a voice per person, a kid-safe model for the child.
  9. v11
    Flat, and every touch answersDepth dropped: “tvOS is for a remote; this is touch.” Every press sinks in and springs back, then does something.
  10. v12

The Stage

The TV

The chat bar becomes the explanation.

Ask “What does ephemeral mean?” and the bar grows over the whole stage: the card on the left, the conversation on the right. Ask another question and the card swaps in place. Close it, or just stop talking for twelve seconds, and it shrinks back to the bar. Your tiles come back exactly where they were.

closeson ×, or after 12 s of quiet
side columnsstay put and keep ticking behind it

It points

“This part.” And it circles it.

While omx talks, a ring moves to the part of the card it's talking about: the meaning, then the example; the compressor, then the coil; the next stop on a map. You follow along from across the kitchen without reading a word.

Live tiles

Tiles that know what matters now.

A tile grows when it becomes urgent and shrinks back when it's done, moving like water, never jumping. Drag one to reorder, pull its corner to resize, drop it on Pinned to keep it. Your own size always beats urgency.

the Stage demo on Linux, three moments from “Try a day” · charms are small drawings made once and reused every year

Charms, not themes

It dresses for the day. Just a little.

No theme picker. omx notices the moment (a school day, a game, Thanksgiving, rain) and hangs one small charm on the card it's about. The library grows every year: the personal touch people miss in a cold dashboard.

Interfaces made on the fly

The model builds the card. omxcode draws it.

You can't build an app for everything a family asks. So omx doesn't. The model describes the card it wants (a word, a diagram, a map, a checklist, a chart) using only omxcode's own components, and the app checks it and draws it natively. Nothing the model sends runs as code: a button only reports a word back.

It speaks AG-UI, the open protocol for agents and apps: the model's words, tool calls and state arrive as standard events. A card already on screen can change without being rebuilt: a small patch moves one tile and the rest stay where they are.

the catalogcards, text, buttons, fields, toggles, sliders, charts, grids, maps, live tiles
checkedevery card against the same catalog the model was given; a bad one is refused and the model told why
changeda tiles tool (promote, add, remove, change a face) or a JSON Patch, without sending the card again
// what the model sends: a render_ui call, as AG-UI tool-call events
{"ui": {"component": "Card", "title": "Tonight", "children": [
  {"component": "Text", "text": "Kick-off 7:30 · Home v Away"},
  {"component": "Toggle", "label": "Remind me at 7:15",

// later, rain is coming: one tile moves, the rest stay put

What it can show

Words, diagrams, maps and the camera.

A word card that writes itself out. A small isometric diagram of how a thing works, each block a part it can point at. A trip on a real map that flies to each stop as it names it. A look through the camera with boxes around what it sees: the camera's pictures never leave the machine. Your notes, searched and cited. The to-do list, the news, a comic.

Local

Your kitchen. Your GPU. Your model.

Every omx answer on this page came from Qwen3.8-27B on an RTX 3090 in the room, inside omx's own process: no server to call, no browser in between. Hearing (Parakeet), speaking (Kokoro) and knowing when you've stopped talking (Silero) run on a second card in the same machine. It goes to a cloud model only if you ask for Claude by name, and then the whole screen gets an orange frame so you always know.

One process holds everything. The model and its memory of the conversation stay warm on the GPU between turns; the cards are checked and laid out on the CPU right beside it, and drawn as the model's words stream in, not after.

0.8 sfrom the end of your question to omx's first spoken word

0.2 sto stop talking when you talk over it

Native everywhere

One Mojo app. Every screen in the house.

omx is written once in Mojo on omxcode, and each platform draws it with its own native controls: GTK on Linux, AppKit on the Mac. No web view. The Stage reflows from three columns on a counter screen to one on a phone.

Try it

Build it on your machine.

The Stage runs anywhere omxcode does, with sample data and canned answers. omx itself needs its models and NVIDIA GPUs: we run it on two RTX 3090s, the model on one and the voice on the other.

git clone https://github.com/abhisheksreesaila/omxcode
cd omxcode && pixi install
# the Stage, with sample data
scripts/build.sh stage linux && build/stage/linux/stage
# omx, with its models
scripts/build.sh omx linux && build/omx/linux/omx
from the source · Linux

omx looks for Qwen3.8-27B-UD-Q4_K_M.gguf and parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3 in OMX_MODELS; the full list of settings is at the top of examples/omx/app.mojo

Honest edges. It's an alpha, and there is no download yet: one comes once the app can carry its models.
omx with its model and voice runs on Linux, measured on two RTX 3090s; a single-card setup hasn't been measured. On the Mac, the Stage runs natively; the model hasn't run there yet.
iPhone and iPad: the host is built but hasn't run on a device yet. Android: not started for omx.
The tiles' calendar, weather, photos and door are sample data; real connectors come later.
A rare crash showed up in long test runs: 3 in 16 before a fix, none in 32 since. Its root cause isn't found yet.
