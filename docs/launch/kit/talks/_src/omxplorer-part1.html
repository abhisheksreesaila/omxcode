A robot is putting a shirt in a box, and the shirt ends up outside it. Why? In this talk I answer that on real public robot data, in one app called omxplorer. Sixty-three thousand episodes from thirteen labs, a question in words over almost seventeen million telemetry rows in about forty milliseconds, and then the moment itself on video. This is part one of three: the story.

Every step is an export or a copy. By the time they line up, the question is gone.

Here's how you'd do it today. A spreadsheet of episodes tells you which ones failed. A video player shows you one. A Python notebook plots the joints and the gripper, and a PDF tells you how the data was recorded. Four apps, with exports between them. I wanted the question to stay in one place.

The data is DROID, a public robot dataset from 2024, released under CC-BY. The public 720p port has 67,029 episodes, about 326 hours, from 13 labs. Each episode has 720p video and 52 telemetry channels at 15 hertz. There's no force sensing in DROID, so I won't show force; I read a drop from the gripper and the video.

Beat 2 · nothing joins until I link it

Four links, typed by a person.

fleet.episode = telemetry.episode   -- key
fleet.clip    = episode.file        -- key → video
telemetry.t   ~ episode.t +0        -- time
fleet.source  = paper.file          -- key → document

The episode key, its video file, the clock, its paper. That's the whole "schema", and it lives on the sheet, not in code.

docs/goals/47-report.md, "Record it yourself", beat 2 Links
Now I connect things, by hand. Four lines: the fleet to its telemetry by episode, the fleet to its video file, the telemetry to the video by time, and the fleet to its paper. Nothing in omxplorer joins by guessing. Part two goes deep on this, because it's the part I like most.

Said plainly

What the data can't tell you.

No force

DROID has no force or torque. "Dropped" is read from the gripper and the video.

Filters, not a detector

Slip and regrasp features don't predict failure across labs: a slip shows in 18 % of successes too.

Inferred tasks

Failures carry no instruction; their task is the nearest success on the same robot, same day.

docs/goals/47-report.md, "What the data doesn't support"
Three honest notes. There's no force data, so I never talk about grip force. The gripper features I compute are good filters, but they don't predict failure across labs. And failure episodes don't come with an instruction, so their task is taken from the nearest success on the same robot that day. The app says so in a column.
